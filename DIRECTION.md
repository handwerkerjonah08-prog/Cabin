# Cabin: direction

## Brief

- **What:** a full marketing site for Cabin, a self-driving car service built for long-distance trips. It competes with Uber and Lyft on price and with Waymo and Tesla on autonomy, and beats all four on one thing: **there is no service area.** A Cabin goes anywhere a road goes, at any hour.
- **For:** people choosing how to make a 200 to 1,400 mile trip: drive themselves, fly, take a bus, or hire a car. Families, couples, people moving a dog and three suitcases, people who hate airports.
- **The one job:** in ten seconds, a visitor understands what Cabin is (a self-driving car for long trips with no service area) and what it offers (cheap, safe, comfortable rides between cities, priced by the mile).
- **Feel:** useful, unique, premium.
- **References:** none given. Self-authored below.
- **Assets:** none. Name, logo, palette, type, copy and data are all designed here. Prices, distances and product details are proposals for the owner to confirm.
- **Imagery:** drawn in code only if it looks good. No paid generation from this session. Where a photo would help, a prompt is written for the owner to generate.

## Imagery we can honestly get

1. The product's own data: real routes, real driving distances, real geography (US Census state shapes via `us-atlas`, projected here).
2. Drawn in code: SVG and canvas, one hand.
3. Owner-generated photos later (prompts in the hand-off). Nothing on the first screen depends on them.

No photos of the car, the interior or riders exist, so the site must not be photo-led.

## The category default we are refusing

The top competitors share:

- **Structure:** a car photo on a sunny city street, a "Where to?" pickup/drop-off field, app store badges, a safety-statistics strip, a service-area map with a shaded polygon over one city.
- **Palette:** white or black ground with electric blue or teal (Waymo), pure black (Tesla, Uber), hot pink (Lyft).
- **Type:** a geometric or neo-grotesque sans in caps (Uber Move, Gotham-likes, Tesla's T-type).
- **Category colour cliché:** electric blue and teal for autonomy and EVs; black for "premium mobility".
- **Slop faces this brief falls into naturally:** the Launch Page ("the future of transportation" on navy with a glow), the Tesla-black cinematic with a stock car photo, and the Paper Edition as the "premium" reflex.

Cabin's difference is distance. Every competitor's map has an edge. Ours doesn't. The site should be about the country, the route and the night, not the city street.

## Three concepts

- **A. Cabin is the moving map on a red-eye's seatback screen.** (media) The whole lower 48 drawn as a field of dots, a little amber car crawling a real interstate, readouts for distance to go and time to arrival. Dark night ground, Archivo at its widest for display with Martian Mono readouts, amber accent, data as the richness source, tool-first grammar (the hero prices a real trip), dot-matrix motion.
- **B. Cabin is the green interstate sign you pass at 2am.** (place) A full field of highway-sign green, reflective white type in Overpass (the open-source descendant of the FHWA's Highway Gothic), destination rows with mileage. Colour-field ground, grotesque type, green accent, shape as the richness source, poster grammar, kinetic type (the mileage counts down as you drive).
- **C. Cabin is the view out a sleeper window at first light.** (nature) A plains landscape engraved in fine contour lines, one ink on a pale morning ground, one thing moving: a Cabin's tail lights on the road to the horizon. Light ground, Gloock display serif with Hanken Grotesk text, tail-light red accent, illustration as the richness source, scroll-story grammar, engraving motion.

| | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| ground | dark | colour-field | light |
| type | expanded (Archivo 125) | grotesque (Overpass) | serif (Gloock) |
| richness | data | shape | illustration |
| accent | yellow (amber) | green | red |
| grammar | tool-first | poster | scroll-story |
| motion | dot matrix | kinetic type | engraving |

## The pick: A, the moving map

**A wins.** It is the only one that shows Cabin's actual difference on the first screen: the whole lower 48 with no edge drawn on it, a real interstate route, the hours it takes and the fare for the car. It is useful (the hero is a working trip pricer), unique (nobody in mobility sells with an in-flight moving map) and premium in the way a red-eye in a lie-flat seat is premium. Put Waymo's logo on it and it reads wrong, because Waymo's map has edges.

- **B lost** on the swap test. A green guide sign belongs to every road trip, every navigation app and every state DOT, so it says "driving" but not "Cabin". It is also rows of destinations with numbers, which is the departure-board rut wearing a different colour.
- **C lost** on information. It is the prettiest of the three and says "overnight comfort" well, but nothing in it says "no service area" or "priced by the mile", and a serif headline with a red accent is one step from the Paper Edition. Its idea survives: the engraved dawn becomes the arrival scene at the end of the page, drawn in the same hand as the map.
